# Oncology + Cancer-Patient/Caregiver AI Landscape
## Q4 2025 – Q2 2026 Competitive Analysis

**Research Date:** May 22, 2026 | **Coverage Period:** November 2025 – May 2026  
**Prepared for:** CareCompanion Product & Strategy Team

---

## Executive Summary

The oncology AI market is growing at 33–34% CAGR, from $2.35B in 2025 to a projected $6.26B by 2030. Despite this growth, a persistent structural gap exists: the overwhelming majority of capital and innovation flows to clinician-facing documentation, pharma R&D tooling, and imaging diagnostics — not to the patient and caregiver layer. Only three commercial consumer-facing cancer AI apps have raised meaningful funding in the past 18 months (Belong.Life, Radical Health, CanSurround), and caregiver-specific AI remains essentially nonexistent as a commercial category.

This gap is CareCompanion's core whitespace opportunity.

## Section 3: Regulatory Landscape (May 2026 Snapshot)

The FDA has authorized 1,350+ AI-enabled medical devices as of May 2026, doubling since 2022. Oncology-specific highlights:

- **Imaging AI cleared:** Lunit INSIGHT MMG + DBT (mammography detection), Ibex Prostate Detect AI (510(k) cleared December 2025)
- **Pending:** Lunit INSIGHT Risk (submitted December 2025; Breakthrough Device Designation April 2025)
- **IVD pathway active:** OncoHost PROphet, Isabl GxT (both Breakthrough Device Designations); Waiv pursuing EU MDR + FDA IVD
- **January 2026 FDA guidance:** Deregulatory stance on CDS and wellness software — broader enforcement discretion for health prediction apps that do not replace clinical judgment. This reduces friction for launching HIPAA-compliant cancer support apps without FDA clearance.
- **Effective regulatory tier for patient/caregiver apps:** HIPAA-covered wellness apps are the operative standard — not FDA SaMD — for symptom tracking, education, trial navigation, and support tools that do not claim diagnostic or treatment use.

## Section 7: Big Tech Threat Assessment

### Google Health
**Threat level: Medium-term, indirect.** The ASCO Guidelines Assistant (May 2025) is a clinician tool, not patient-facing. Med-Gemini's consumer deployment remains undirected at cancer patients specifically. Google's footprint is institutional — requiring ASCO membership, health system integration. CareCompanion's moat is direct consumer relationship and caregiver specificity, which Google has no commercial incentive to build.

### Microsoft / Nuance Dragon Copilot
**Threat level: Low (different user).** Pure clinician ambient documentation play. No patient/caregiver product. Distribution entirely through Epic and health systems. Dragon Copilot's expansion actually creates an opportunity: if oncology notes are better structured, downstream patient-facing summarization (CareCompanion's use case) becomes higher quality.

### Amazon / One Medical
**Threat level: Medium.** One Medical Health AI Assistant (Jan 2026) is general-purpose, not oncology-specific, and requires One Medical membership (~$9/month primary care). The AWS Agentic Breast Cancer tool is a demo, not a shipping consumer product. Risk scenario: Amazon bundles a cancer-specific module into One Medical — but their track record in oncology-specific consumer product development is poor.

### Epic (Emmie)
**Threat level: High (structural, slow).** Epic's Emmie in MyChart is the most credible patient-facing threat — embedded in 600+ health systems, zero marginal acquisition cost. However, Emmie is generic (lab results, scheduling, screening reminders) and health-system controlled. Epic cannot build caregiver tools, cannot target unaffiliated patients, and cannot iterate as fast as a startup. CareCompanion's advantage is speed, caregiver specificity, and AI depth.

## Section 9: CareCompanion Whitespace Analysis

### The Unfilled Quadrant: Consumer/Wellness × Patient+Caregiver

The research confirms that the consumer patient/caregiver layer of oncology AI has received less than 2% of the capital flowing into oncology AI overall. Specific capability gaps confirmed as commercially unaddressed:

**1. Caregiver-specific AI (fully unaddressed)**  
No commercial product has launched targeting the family caregiver of a cancer patient as the primary user. Caregivers are estimated to provide 32.9 billion hours of unpaid cancer care annually in the US (AARP/NAC data). CanSurround gestures toward "caregiver support" but does not differentiate UX or AI features for caregivers vs. patients. Lantern uses human navigators, not AI. This is the clearest whitespace.

**2. Consumer-grade side-effect tracking with AI inference**  
Carevive delivers ePRO-based symptom tracking but is health-system-owned, clinician-monitored, and inaccessible without provider enrollment. No app lets a patient independently log side effects, receive AI-powered severity assessment, and get actionable guidance (hydration, nutrition, when to call the care team) without a health system intermediary. The January 2026 FDA deregulatory guidance makes this launchable as a wellness app without FDA clearance.

**3. Treatment summarization for patients/caregivers**  
Radical Health generates AI treatment option reports, but these are technical documents aimed at informed patients making treatment decisions. No product converts the oncologist's clinical note, pathology report, or treatment plan into plain-language summaries for the patient's caregiver — the person most likely to be managing logistics, remembering instructions, and communicating with extended family.

**4. Symptom triage with oncology-specific urgency calibration**  
General triage apps (Infermedica, K Health) exist, but none are calibrated for immunocompromised cancer patients where "mild fever" has entirely different urgency than in a healthy adult. No consumer-facing triage product exists with oncology-specific severity thresholds.

### CareCompanion's Positioning Recommendation

**Positioning:** AI-native care companion for cancer patients and their caregivers — the first product built for the caregiver as a first-class user, not a secondary one.

**Differentiation from existing players:**

| Player | Why CareCompanion Differentiates |
|--------|----------------------------------|
| Belong.Life | Social network model → engagement platform; CareCompanion is a utility + AI coach; no caregiver UX |
| Radical Health | Treatment decision support (complex); CareCompanion is daily care management (simple, high-frequency) |
| CanSurround | Stagnant funding; wellness-only (meditation, journaling); no AI inference layer |
| Lantern | Human navigators + employer channel; not AI-native; not accessible without employer benefit |
| Epic Emmie | Health-system-controlled; no caregiver access; no standalone mobile app |
| Carevive | Clinician-ordered; health system pays; patient is a passive data source, not the customer |

**Defensible moat:** First-mover in caregiver AI + longitudinal cancer journey data → proprietary dataset for future pharma/life sciences data licensing (Belong.Life's Tempus/IQVIA monetization model, replicated for caregivers).

**Distribution path:** B2B2C via cancer center patient education programs + employer benefits (Lantern model) → direct consumer via App Store, targeting caregivers at diagnosis moment.

**Revenue model:** Freemium consumer → payer/employer subscription → pharma data licensing (patient-reported outcomes, side-effect signal).
